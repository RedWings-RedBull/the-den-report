## Where the standings stand

After Week 11, the NFC North has a three-way tie at the top. The Detroit Lions, Minnesota Vikings and Chicago Bears are all 7-2, and the standings table lists head-to-head as the tiebreaker separating them. The Green Bay Packers are 6-4 and sit fourth, with no tiebreaker in play.

The tie hides real differences in how each club got there.

- **Detroit** has the longest streak, W5, and a 2-0 division record. Its 257 points scored lead the division, and its point differential is +58.
- **Chicago** is W4 with a +57 differential (241 for, 184 against). The Bears are 0-2 in the division, so they have yet to beat a rival.
- **Minnesota** is W1 and has the best division record of the three at 3-1. Its +50 differential comes from 176 points scored and 126 allowed, the fewest allowed in the division.
- **Green Bay** has lost its most recent game (L1). It is 1-3 in the division with a +5 differential (226 for, 221 against), far behind the three leaders.

Minnesota is the favorite for the division title and has the best playoff odds. Detroit is second for the division but has the lowest playoff probability of the three leaders. Chicago is slightly more likely than Detroit to make the playoffs (89.9% to 88.6%) but has a 24.4% chance of winning the division against Detroit's 34.5%. The expected-win totals for the three sit between 11.5 and 11.8, so the simulation sees them as close.

Green Bay's 1.1% division chance effectively makes it a wild-card contender. A 33.9% playoff chance means it is more likely to miss than to make it.

## Does efficiency back up the records?

The efficiency numbers, measured in expected points added (EPA) per play, are less even than the records.

**Minnesota** is the most unusual case. Its defense ranks first at -0.128 EPA per play. Its offense ranks 29th at -0.100. The defense is why the 7-2 record holds up, and the offense leaves little cushion if the defense slips.

**Detroit** is the reverse. The Lions' offense is sixth at +0.107 per play, the best offensive figure in the division. The defense is 24th at +0.041, and a positive figure means it is giving up value. That fits the 199 points allowed, the most among the top three. Detroit's record rests on its offense.

**Chicago** has no outlier. The offense is 10th (+0.039) and the defense is eighth (-0.023). Those balanced numbers fit a +57 differential. The 0-2 division record is the one mark against the Bears' case.

**Green Bay** has a better profile than its record suggests. Its defense is seventh (-0.039) and its offense is 14th (+0.012). That efficiency is respectable, but the +5 differential and 9.3 expected wins show the Packers have not turned it into margin.
