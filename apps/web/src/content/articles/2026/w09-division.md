## Where the standings sit

Ten weeks in, the Chicago Bears lead the NFC North at 7-2 with a four-game winning streak and the division's best point differential, +57 (241 scored, 184 allowed). The catch is their divisional record: 0-2, the only zero in the group.

The Detroit Lions are 6-2 and 2-0 inside the division, also on a four-game winning streak, with a +33 differential. The Minnesota Vikings are 6-2 as well, 2-1 in the division, but have lost two straight. Their +30 comes from 146 points scored and 116 allowed. The table lists head-to-head as the tiebreaker for both the Lions (second) and the Vikings (third). The Green Bay Packers are 6-3, 1-2 in the division, on a two-game winning streak, with a +25 differential (216 for, 191 against).

Chicago, Detroit and Minnesota each have two losses. Green Bay has three, and its 1-2 divisional mark is the weakest of the group apart from Chicago's 0-2.

## What the simulations say

Across 10,000 simulation runs, the division is close to a three-way race:

- **Bears:** 31.5% to win the division, 89.9% to make the playoffs, 11.5 expected wins.
- **Lions:** 29.5% division, 78.5% playoffs, 11 expected wins.
- **Vikings:** 28.1% division, 83.8% playoffs, 11 expected wins.
- **Packers:** 11% division, 66.5% playoffs, 10.3 expected wins.

Chicago is the favorite, but not by much: 31.5% is only two points ahead of Detroit. Minnesota's playoff number sits above Detroit's even though Detroit has the slightly better division probability, which suggests the model sees the Vikings as a better bet to reach the playoffs without winning the title. Green Bay's division chances are thin, yet a 66.5% playoff probability keeps it in the conversation.

## Does efficiency back up the records?

**Bears.** The point differential is the best in the division, but the efficiency numbers are good rather than dominant. Chicago's offense is 13th at 0.037 EPA per play, and its defense is 10th at -0.049. The record and scoring margin run ahead of that per-play profile, and the 0-2 divisional record leaves the lead less secure than 7-2 suggests.

**Lions.** This is the most lopsided profile. The offense is sixth at 0.102 EPA per play, but the defense ranks 28th at 0.080. The offense supports the 6-2 record. The defense is the weak point.

**Vikings.** The widest gap between record and process. Minnesota's offense ranks 31st at -0.162 EPA per play. The defense carries the team: second at -0.149. A 6-2 record built on that profile, plus a current two-game skid, makes the 83.8% playoff figure look generous for an offense this far down the rankings. The defense is the reason the number holds up.

**Packers.** Green Bay has the most balanced numbers of the four: offense 12th at 0.038, defense sixth at -0.076. That profile is at least as sturdy as Chicago's, which suggests the 6-3 record and 11% division odds understate the team somewhat. Its 1-2 divisional record is the obstacle, and it raises the stakes for the game below.
