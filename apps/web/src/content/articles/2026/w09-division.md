## Where the standings stand

Chicago sits first at 7-2 with a four-game winning streak and a +57 point differential (241 scored, 184 allowed). That is the best margin in the division. The Bears also carry its one clear blemish: an 0-2 record against NFC North opponents. Every other club has at least one division win.

Detroit is second at 6-2, also on a four-game winning streak, and is 2-0 in the division. The Lions' differential is +33 (222 for, 189 against). Minnesota is third at 6-2 with a +30 margin (146 for, 116 against) and a 2-1 division mark. The standings list both clubs with a head-to-head tiebreaker, and Detroit sits ahead of Minnesota. The Vikings have lost two straight.

Green Bay is fourth at 6-3 and 1-2 in the division, with a +25 differential (216 for, 191 against) and a two-game winning streak. The Packers trail the 6-2 clubs by a game in the loss column.

## What the simulations say

The odds come from 10,000 simulation runs. The top three are bunched, and Green Bay sits well back in the division race.

- **Chicago:** 31.4% to win the division, 89.9% to make the playoffs, 11.5 expected wins.
- **Detroit:** 29.7% division, 78.7% playoffs, 11 expected wins.
- **Minnesota:** 27.8% division, 83.5% playoffs, 11 expected wins.
- **Green Bay:** 11.1% division, 66.8% playoffs, 10.3 expected wins.

Chicago, Detroit and Minnesota are separated by only a few points of division probability, so the race is close to a three-way toss-up. Chicago's lead in the standings translates to only a small edge in the simulation.

Minnesota's playoff number (83.5%) is higher than Detroit's (78.7%), even though Detroit has the better division chance. Green Bay is a long shot for the title, but the model gives it a 66.8% chance of reaching the postseason.

## Does efficiency back up the records?

**Chicago** has the best margin in the division without standout efficiency. The Bears' offense is 13th in EPA per play (0.037) and their defense is 10th (-0.049, where negative is better). Those are solid but unspectacular marks. They don't fully explain the best point differential in the division, so Chicago's record looks a little ahead of its underlying play.

**Detroit** has the most lopsided profile. The offense is sixth at 0.102 EPA per play, the best offensive mark in the division. The defense is 28th, at 0.08 EPA allowed per play, the only positive defensive figure in the group. The 6-2 record rests on the offense, and the defense is the obvious vulnerability.

**Minnesota** is the reverse. The defense is second in the league at -0.149 EPA per play, and that unit is the reason a 6-2 record is believable. The offense is 31st at -0.162, the weakest offensive number here by a wide margin. That matches the two-game losing streak. The Vikings' 146 points scored is the lowest in the division, but their 116 allowed is the lowest too. They have also played fewer games than Chicago and Green Bay, so the totals understate their per-game pace.

**Green Bay** is the most balanced. The offense is 12th at 0.038 and the defense is sixth at -0.076. That is nearly identical to Chicago offensively and better defensively. The Packers' 11.1% division chance looks low against that profile, but a 1-2 division record and three losses leave little margin for error.
